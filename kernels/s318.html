What happens

The R4000 has no conditional moves (movn/movz/movt arrived with MIPS IV), so MIPSpro if-converts the update with branch-likely: bc1tl .+8 executes its delay slot only when the FP condition is true, and the next PC is .+8 either way.

In r9999 a branch-likely that is not taken nullifies its delay slot by restarting the pipeline. Every likely form in exec.sv has

t_mispred_br = (int_uop.br_pred != t_take_br) || !t_take_br;

so a not-taken branch-likely is a misprediction whatever was predicted. For a max reduction the predicate is almost always false. All four steady-state bc1tl restart on nearly every execution, 4900 times each. The next instruction is fetched about 17 cycles after the branch:

8002cff8  bc1tl 0x8002d000   F   1 A   6 C  13 R  15
8002d000  addiu $at,$at,1    F  18 A  25 C  29 R  30     <- fetch restarts after the bc1tl resolves
8002d004  abs.s $f2,$f2      F  18 A  25 C  29 R  30
8002d008  bc1tl 0x8002d010   F  19 A  26 C  30 R  31
8002d010  beq $a1,$at,...    F  34 A  40 C  44 R  45     <- and again

Each trip handles 2 elements with 4 restarts and takes about 72 cycles. The nullified delay slots never retire, so they don't appear in the trace. The no-SWP loop's ordinary bc1f branches are learned by the predictor, fetch runs well ahead, and that loop is limited instead by its FP dependence chain: c.lt.s completes every 12 cycles per 2 elements.

s3110, s273 and s315 lose to SWP the same way.

Not a scheduling problem. To make MIPSpro's R4000 predication cheap, the delay slot of a likely branch could be renamed as a predicated op that selects the old destination value when the branch is not taken, instead of restarting the pipeline.
